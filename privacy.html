Privacy Notice

Last updated: 5 October 2026

This notice explains what personal data Codebase Time Machine processes, why, on what legal basis, and for how long. It covers visitors to this website and clients of our repository analysis service (Art. 13 GDPR).

Controller

Josiah Shirt, trading as Codebase Time Machine, Bremen, Germany. Email: hello@codebasetimemachine.com. You'll reach a person, not a support queue.

Visiting this website

  • Hosting. This website is hosted on GitHub Pages (GitHub, Inc., USA). When you load a page, GitHub processes your IP address, the time of the request, the page requested and your browser's user-agent, to deliver the page and keep the service secure. Legal basis: our legitimate interest in operating a secure website (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR). GitHub decides how long it keeps these logs; we have no access to them. For transfers to the USA, the safeguards are as described in GitHub's own privacy statement.
  • Fonts and scripts are served from this website itself. No request is made to Google Fonts or any other third-party content network when you load a page.
  • No cookies, analytics or tracking. This website sets no cookies and uses no analytics scripts or advertising pixels.

Booking a call

The "Book a call" buttons link to Calendly (Calendly LLC, USA). If you book, Calendly processes your name, email address, the time you choose and any answers you give, and passes them to us so we can hold the call. Legal basis: steps taken at your request before a contract (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR). For transfers to the USA, the safeguards are as described in Calendly's own privacy notice, which applies on its pages. We keep booking details for as long as the conversation is active, and delete them within 12 months of our last contact unless an engagement follows.

Contacting us by email

If you email us, we process what you send, typically your name, email address and message. Our email is hosted by IONOS SE, Germany. Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR where you ask about an engagement, otherwise our legitimate interest in answering you (Art. 6(1)(f)). We delete the correspondence when it is no longer needed, at the latest 12 months after our last contact, unless we must keep it for legal reasons (for example, commercial and tax retention duties).

The repository analysis service

When you engage us, we process only what is needed to run the analysis:

  • Git commit history and metadata from the repository you grant access to: commit authors (names and email addresses), dates, file paths and commit messages.
  • A read-only access token scoped to that repository, used for the duration of the scan. It is sent to your repository host as an authorisation header and is never written into the clone's configuration, a command line, a log or any report.
  • Contact and billing details you give us (company name, email, engagement scope).

Purpose and legal basis. To produce the report you have asked for (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR towards you as our client). For the contributors whose names appear in the commit history, we act on your instructions as your processor, and we can sign a data processing agreement under Art. 28 GDPR.

Where it runs. The repository is cloned onto the machine running the analysis, into a temporary directory created for that scan. Before an engagement we tell you, in writing, which machine and which providers (if any) are involved, and list them in the data processing agreement.

AI. The optional AI narrative is off by default. With it off, no part of your repository, no commit message and no contributor identity reaches any AI service. We enable it only if you agree in writing first.

Pseudonymisation. By default, reports identify contributors as "Contributor A", "Contributor B", with no names or email addresses. This is pseudonymisation, not anonymisation.

Retention and deletion

  • The cloned repository and the temporary report files are deleted when the scan ends, whether it succeeds or fails, and each deletion is recorded in a log that contains no file names, paths or contributor identities. A forcibly terminated scan can leave a temporary copy behind without recording it; we check by hand after any forcibly terminated scan, and delete what we find.
  • The delivered PDF and Excel report are kept for the period agreed with you for that engagement and then deleted. You can ask for earlier deletion at any time.
  • The deletion record is kept as evidence that deletion happened, for as long as we keep the engagement's records. It contains a scan identifier, the class of data removed, the result and the time; no file names, paths or contributor identities.
  • Contact and billing details for an engagement are kept for the statutory retention periods under German commercial and tax law (HGB and AO), then deleted. Legal basis: our legal obligation to keep them (Art. 6(1)(c) GDPR).
  • We keep no copy of your source code beyond the scan, apart from a copy a forcibly terminated scan may leave, which we delete when we find it.

Who receives your data

The providers named above: GitHub (website hosting), Calendly (call bookings) and IONOS SE, Germany (email). For an engagement, any provider that hosts the analysis is named to you in writing before it starts and listed in the data processing agreement. We do not sell, rent or share your data for marketing.

Do you have to provide your data?

No law requires you to give us personal data. Without your name and email address we cannot book a call or answer you, and without read access to the repository we cannot run the analysis.

Automated decision-making

We make no decisions about you based solely on automated processing, including profiling (Art. 22 GDPR). The report measures commit history; any conclusion about a person is for you and the company's management to confirm.

Your rights

You have the right to access (Art. 15 GDPR), rectification (Art. 16), erasure (Art. 17), restriction of processing (Art. 18), data portability (Art. 20), and to object to processing based on our legitimate interests (Art. 21). To exercise them, email hello@codebasetimemachine.com.

You also have the right to complain to a data protection supervisory authority. The authority responsible for us is: Die Landesbeauftragte für Datenschutz und Informationsfreiheit der Freien Hansestadt Bremen.

What we do not claim

We hold no certification (no SOC 2, no ISO 27001, no GDPR certification) and do not encrypt repository data at rest. Our Security & Trust Pack states our controls and open gaps; ask us for it.
