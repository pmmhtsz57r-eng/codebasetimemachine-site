01Why it mattersKey-person risk is a deal question.

CTM adds one question to technical diligence: where the critical code knowledge sits, and whether whole subsystems depend on one or two people who may have no contractual reason to stay past close.

If the one person who understands a core subsystem leaves the day after you sign, what breaks, how long until anyone else can safely touch it, and what does that do to your model?

Value at risk

If only one engineer can safely change a subsystem, the value you are paying for depends on someone who can resign the week after close.

Integration drag

A single bottleneck engineer can delay a migration, carve-out or roadmap after the price is already set.

Deal leverage

Raised and confirmed before signing, a concentration can shape a retention package or an earn-out condition instead of becoming a post-close surprise.

02What we measureHow a file is flagged, with a live example.

Where code knowledge is concentrated, file by file.

We measure what share of each file's changed lines (added and removed, across its whole history) one contributor wrote. That line share is the evidence. A tier is then derived from the line share and the file's commit count against fixed thresholds, not a subjective judgment.

Illustrative data, not a real repository. Each tile is a subsystem, shown with the tier and line share of its most concentrated file. In a report, tiers are assigned per file; subsystems get their own concentration measures.

Three tiers, from fixed thresholds, not guessed.

High-confidence: 10+ commits on a file, with one contributor writing over 70% of its changed lines (held at Watch when a single commit accounts for most of that contributor's lines). Strong evidence of concentration; confirm with management who maintains it today.

Watch: 5+ commits and over 55% line share. Concentration is visible but the history is shorter; ask who maintains it today.

Distributed: no single contributor reaches the Watch threshold.

A file with fewer than 5 commits gets no tier, however concentrated it is: so few commits describe a few changes, not a pattern of who holds the knowledge.

05Security & your dataRead-only, AI off, pseudonymised by default.

Your source code is copied for one scan. The copy is deleted when the scan ends, or by hand if the scan is forcibly terminated.

The automatic deletion is recorded. A forcibly terminated scan can leave a temporary copy behind without recording it, so after one we check by hand and delete what we find. CTM is a tool we run for you, not a cloud service you sign up to: there is no customer account and no database holding your repository.

✓

How a scan works. Your repository is cloned over HTTPS onto the machine running the analysis, into a temporary directory created for that one scan. It is read there and nowhere else. When the scan ends, whether it succeeds or fails, that directory is deleted, along with the temporary directory used to assemble the report. What survives is the report we hand you, and a deletion log (unless the scan was forcibly terminated; see below).

✓

What leaves that machine. The authenticated request to your repository host to fetch the code; a metadata request to that host's API; the finished report, which we deliver to you; and, if you ask for it, the deletion record. Nothing else, unless you have agreed in writing to the optional AI narrative, which is off by default.

✓

The optional AI narrative is off. With it off, no part of your repository, no commit message and no contributor identity reaches any AI service. Every number and every finding in the report is computed deterministically from your repository's files, its Git history and repository metadata on the machine that ran the scan. If we ever propose enabling it, we will ask you first and in writing.

✓

Identities are pseudonymised by default. Unless you ask in writing for named reports, contributor names and email addresses are replaced with pseudonyms such as "Contributor A". Names that appear inside file paths, commit messages or code-owner team handles are not scrubbed. (Pseudonymised, not anonymised: this reduces exposure, it does not take the data outside GDPR.)

✓

Every report records what produced it. Each report carries its own provenance: the exact version of CTM, whether that version had local modifications, the analysis settings, the timestamp, and whether AI was used.

✓

Deletion is evidenced. Each automatic removal of temporary data is recorded: which scan, which class of data, whether it succeeded, and when. We can send you that record. It deliberately contains no file names, no paths and no contributor identities, so the evidence of deletion never becomes a place your information comes to rest. A forcibly terminated scan leaves no automatic record; see below.

✓

Credentials. You give us a read-only token scoped to the repository you want analysed, which you can revoke at any time. It is sent to your repository host as an HTTP authorisation header and is never written into the clone's configuration, a command line, a log, or any report.

✓

Supported hosts. github.com, gitlab.com and bitbucket.org. GitHub Enterprise Server and self-managed GitLab are not supported today.

What we do not claim

We would rather tell you this than have you find it out:

–

No encryption at rest. Your code is protected while it exists by the short lifetime of the copy and by the machine's own access controls, not by application-level encryption.

–

No certification. No SOC 2, no ISO 27001, no GDPR certification, and no independent penetration test.

–

Access to the tool is protected by an operator passphrase, not individual accounts or single sign-on.

–

A forcibly terminated scan can leave a temporary copy behind without recording that it did. We check by hand after any forcibly terminated scan.

Working with European customers. We can sign a data processing agreement under Art. 28 GDPR. Ask us for the Security & Trust Pack: it covers the data flow, retention and deletion, access control, incident response, AI egress, subprocessors, and a GDPR readiness checklist that states the open gaps as plainly as the controls.

07FAQCost, timing, access and AI.
What does Codebase Time Machine actually analyze?

The full repository is cloned for one scan: its source files and its Git history (authors, dates, file paths, commit messages and the content of each change), plus basic metadata from your repository host. CTM counts and attributes lines, and scans file contents and changes for exposed secrets and licence files. Source code can contain personal data, and we treat it that way. Not runtime data, and not anyone who never committed code. Git shows who wrote and changed the code; who maintains it today is a question for management, and the report says so.

Do you need write access to our repo?

No. Access is read-only, through a token scoped to the repository, and you can revoke it at any time.

What happens to our code after the scan?

Your repository is copied for a single scan and the copy is deleted when the scan ends, with the deletion recorded (a forcibly terminated scan is the exception; we check by hand after one). The optional AI narrative is off by default, so nothing goes to any AI service. Delivered reports pseudonymise contributor identities by default, and each report states the exact tooling and settings that produced it. We hold no certification and do not encrypt at rest. Our full Security & Trust Pack says so, along with everything else we do and do not do.

How is "risk" actually calculated?

Tiers come from fixed thresholds on line share and commit count. For example, 10+ commits on a file with one contributor writing over 70% of its changed lines is High-confidence, unless a single commit accounts for most of those lines. It's not a subjective score, and it measures concentration of history, not who maintains the code today.

What does it cost?

Every engagement is quoted individually, before you grant any access, so you know exactly where you stand up front. Book a call or email us and we'll turn a quote around quickly.

How long does a scan take?

The analysis runs in minutes to a few hours, depending on repository size and depth. Depth is your call, from the most recent commits up to the repository's complete history, and the report states the exact share of the history it analysed.

What does AI do in the report?

Nothing, by default. Every number and finding is computed deterministically from your repository's files, its Git history and repository metadata. An optional AI narrative exists; we enable it only if you agree in writing first.
