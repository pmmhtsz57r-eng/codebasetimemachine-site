The gap

Code-quality scanners answer the wrong question.

Most technical diligence checks whether the code is well-written. It rarely checks where the critical knowledge actually sits — or whether entire subsystems depend on one or two people who have no contractual reason to stay past close.

If the one person who understands a core subsystem leaves the day after you sign — what breaks, how long until anyone else can safely touch it, and what does that do to your model?

Value at risk

A subsystem only one person can safely change is value you're paying for that can resign the week after close.

Integration drag

A single bottleneck engineer can push a migration, carve-out, or roadmap back by quarters — after the price is already set.

Deal leverage

Surfaced before signing, concentration becomes a retention package, an earn-out clause, or a price adjustment — not a post-close surprise.

What we actually measure

Where technical knowledge is concentrated — across every subsystem.

Commit history is the evidence; concentrated know-how is the finding. Every material area of the codebase gets a bus-factor tier calibrated against measured thresholds — not a subjective judgment. Where one person carries too much of a subsystem's history, the map turns red.

High-confidence (>70% share) Watch (>55% share) Distributed

Three tiers, calibrated — not guessed.

High-confidence — 10+ commits in a subsystem with one person holding over 70% of its history. Treat as a real key-person risk.

Watch — 5+ commits and over 55% share. Concentration is real but history is still short; interview the person who carries it.

Distributed — the knowledge is spread. No single point of failure at close.

FAQ

What people ask before their first scan.

What does Codebase Time Machine actually analyze?

Only Git commit history and metadata — authors, dates, file paths, and commit messages. Not runtime data, not business logic beyond what's visible in the repo itself.

Do you need write access to our repo?

No. Access is always read-only — via a GitHub App install, a scoped token, or a read collaborator invite — and it's revocable by you at any time.

What happens to our code after the scan?

The repo is cloned into a dedicated temporary workspace for the scan, then deleted immediately once the dossier is generated. Nothing is retained.

How is "risk" actually calculated?

Bus-factor tiers are calibrated against commit-history thresholds — for example, 10+ commits on a file with one author holding over 70% share is High-confidence. It's not a subjective score.

What does it cost?

Every engagement is quoted individually, before you grant any access — so you know exactly where you stand up front. Book a call or email us and we'll turn a quote around quickly.

How long does a scan take?

Typically minutes to a few hours, depending on repo size and depth. Depth is your call — from a recent slice up to the repository's complete history — and the dossier states the exact share analyzed. We replay history; we don't sample it.

Can we see a sample before committing our own repo?

Yes — expand the sample dossier above, generated against React's public history. Email us for the full PDF + Excel workbook.
